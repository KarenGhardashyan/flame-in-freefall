## 1. NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) & Spaceflight Flight Experiments

### NASA PSI-117: FLEX Cool Flames CFD (Principal Investigation)
* **Title:** Flame Extinction with Cool Flames - Computational Fluid Dynamics Analysis (FLEX Cool Flames CFD)
* **Principal Investigator / Team:** NASA Physical Sciences Research Program
* **Repository:** NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) System
* **Investigation ID:** PSI-117
* **DOI:** [10.60555/gz0q-b577](https://doi.org/10.60555/gz0q-b577)
* **URL:** [https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-117](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-117)
* **Flight Data & CFD:** 3D CFD and ISS CIR flight telemetry for droplet combustion of n-alkanes (n-heptane, n-decane). Demonstrates radiative extinction of hot flame followed by quasi-steady low-temperature cool flames (600–800 K), zero visible luminescence, carbon monoxide (CO) emission, oscillation modes, and transition back to hot flame under thermoconvective disturbances.
* **Citation:**  
  NASA Physical Sciences Informatics System. (2020). *FLEX Cool Flames CFD (Investigation PSI-117)*. NASA Biological and Physical Sciences Division. https://doi.org/10.60555/gz0q-b577

### NASA Saffire V: Spacecraft Fire Safety Experiments (Full-Scale In-Flight Burn)
* **Title:** Saffire-V Spacecraft Fire Safety Experiment: Large-Scale PMMA Combustion Telemetry and Environmental Analysis
* **Authors:** Niehaus, J., Johnston, M., Urban, D., Ruff, G. A., & NASA Glenn Research Center Saffire Team
* **Conference Paper / Report:** ICES-2024-310 (48th International Conference on Environmental Systems)
* **Local Archive:** `Resources/Saffire_V_ICES_2024_310.pdf`
* **Experimental Platform & Environment:**
  - Vehicle: Northrop Grumman Cygnus pressurized cargo module (Volume: 19.2 m³) post-unberth from the ISS.
  - Atmosphere: Hypobaric, oxygen-enriched environment: Pressure P = 0.71–0.725 atm (71.9–73.4 kPa), O₂ = 26.5%–29.8% by volume.
  - Flow: Saffire Flow Unit (SFU) forced convective co-flow draft: v = 20.0 cm/s (0.20 m/s).
  - Fuel sample: Thick polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) slab in concurrent / opposed flow configurations.
  - Sensor Array: 6 remote atmospheric sensors (RS1–RS6) + Fast Flame Detector (FFD) + thermocouple combs.
  - Measured Excursions: Cabin CO₂ increase of +2230 to +2500 ppm, stoichiometric yield of 1.76 kg CO₂ / kg PMMA consumed, effective convective heat transfer coefficient h = 150 W/(m²·K).
* **Significance:** Empirical operational validation for spacecraft ECLSS scrubber capacity and atmospheric cabin fire dynamics in realistic microgravity vehicle scale.

### NASA PSI-10: ACME / Flame Design
* **Title:** Advanced Combustion via Microgravity Experiments: Flame Design
* **Repository:** NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) System
* **Investigation ID:** PSI-10
* **DOI:** [10.60555/acme-flame-10](https://doi.org/10.60555/acme-flame-10)
* **URL:** [https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-10](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-10)
* **Data & Telemetry:** Soot formation and inception limits of spherical diffusion flames on the ISS CIR as a function of burner flow draft velocity, stoichiometry, and nitrogen (N₂) diluent concentration. Shows clear extinction thresholds when diluent suppresses chemical kinetics below critical Damköhler number.

### NASA PSI-60: SPICE (Smoke Point In Co-flow Experiment — Soot 1g vs 0g)
* **Title:** Smoke Point In Co-flow Experiment: Soot Formation and Radiative Divergence (1g vs 0g)
* **Repository:** NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) System
* **Investigation ID:** PSI-60
* **DOI:** [10.60555/spice-soot-60](https://doi.org/10.60555/spice-soot-60)
* **URL:** [https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-60](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-60)
* **Data & Telemetry:** Quantitative discrepancy in soot volume fraction (f_v) and spatial temperature fields between terrestrial 1g buoyant flames and microgravity 0g diffusion envelopes. In 0g, soot residence time increases by an order of magnitude, producing dense soot shells and dominant radiative heat loss.

### NASA PSI-179: Aerosol Sampling Experiment (ASE — ECLSS Dust vs Soot Dynamics)
* **Title:** Aerosol Sampling Experiment (ASE) in the International Space Station Environment
* **Repository:** NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) System
* **Investigation ID:** PSI-179
* **URL:** [https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-179](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-179)
* **Data & Telemetry:** Characterization of aerosol size distributions in microgravity habitats. Because buoyant thermal plumes are absent in 0g, smoke and coarse dust particles (skin flakes, clothing lint, food particulates) do not settle onto decks or rise to ceilings. Instead, they remain indefinitely suspended in cabin air loops and accumulate at ECLSS return grilles, necessitating multi-wavelength optical discrimination to filter false alarms.

## 2. Foundational Scientific Monograph

* **Monograph:** Law, Chung K. (2006). *Combustion Physics*. Cambridge University Press. ISBN: 978-0521870528.
  * **Section 0.1: Defense and Space Applications:** Spacecraft cabin fire safety, absence of buoyancy-induced venting, failure mode of conventional ceiling smoke detectors, and the fundamental non-flammability criterion for spacecraft cabins (mole fraction of O₂ < 15% even at elevated cabin pressures).
  * **Chapter 6.4: Droplet Vaporization and Combustion:** Spherical symmetry in microgravity, classic d²-law evaporation dynamics, burning rate constant K, Spalding transfer number B, and flame standoff ratio r_f / r_s.
  * **Chapter 8: Limit Phenomena and Chemical Kinetics:** Damköhler number (Da) S-curve formulation, ignition/extinction branch bifurcation, and radiative quenching thresholds.
  * **Local Archive:** `Resources/Law_Combustion_Physics.pdf`

## 4. Open-Source Software Stack

* **NumPy & SciPy:** Scientific matrix operations and numerical integration (Harris et al., Virtanen et al.)
